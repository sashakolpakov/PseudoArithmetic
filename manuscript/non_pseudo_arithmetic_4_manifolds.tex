\title{Not all hyperbolic \(4\)-manifolds are pseudo-arithmetic}
\author{Alexander Kolpakov}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We exhibit three finite-volume hyperbolic Coxeter lattices in dimension four
whose adjoint trace fields are not totally real.  They are the reflection
groups of the Ma--Zheng polytopes \(P_{11,6},P_{11,7},P_{11,8}\).  The
obstruction is visible in only two cyclic products of \(A=2G\), where \(G\)
is the unit-normal Gram matrix.  These products generate elements
\(s=\sqrt5\) and \(u\) satisfying
\[
 (s-1)u^2+2(s-3)u+s-7=0.
\]
At the conjugate place \(s\mapsto-s\), the discriminant is
\(8(1-\sqrt5)<0\).  Thus the adjoint trace field has a complex place, whereas
the adjoint trace field of a pseudo-arithmetic lattice in dimension greater
than three is totally real.  Passing to torsion-free orientation-preserving
finite-index subgroups gives noncompact finite-volume hyperbolic
\(4\)-manifolds that are not pseudo-arithmetic.  This answers two questions of
Emery--Mila in the negative.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The adjoint trace field and the ambient algebraic group are basic
commensurability invariants of a finite-volume hyperbolic manifold.  Emery and
Mila introduced pseudo-arithmetic lattices to encompass both arithmetic
lattices and the nonarithmetic lattices produced by the standard gluing
constructions \cite{EmeryMila}.  They asked whether every lattice in
\(\PO(n,1)\), for \(n>3\), is pseudo-arithmetic and, more weakly, whether its
adjoint trace field must be totally real
\cite[Questions 1.8 and 1.10]{EmeryMila}.

Both questions have negative answers already in dimension four.  The
examples come from Ma and Zheng's classification of the \(331\) finite-volume
hyperbolic Coxeter \(4\)-polytopes with seven facets \cite{MaZheng}.  We use
three consecutive rows of their table.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let \(P_{11,j}\), \(j=6,7,8\), be the Ma--Zheng finite-volume hyperbolic
Coxeter \(4\)-polytopes described in Table~\ref{tab:three-polytopes}, and let
\(\Gamma_{11,j}<\PO(4,1)\) be their reflection groups.  Then the adjoint trace
field of every \(\Gamma_{11,j}\) is not totally real.  Consequently none of
these lattices is pseudo-arithmetic.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:manifolds}
There exist orientable, noncompact, finite-volume hyperbolic
\(4\)-manifolds that are not pseudo-arithmetic.
\end{corollary}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is deliberately short.  For the normalized
Gram matrix \(G\), put \(A=2G\).  One two-cycle in \(A\) recovers
\(\sqrt5\), and one three-cycle recovers an element \(u\).  A quadratic
relation for \(u\) then has negative discriminant at the nontrivial real place
of \(\Q(\sqrt5)\).  This argument uses the field generated by cyclic products,
which is the adjoint trace field of a finite-volume Coxeter reflection group;
it does not confuse that field with the generally larger field generated by
the individual Gram entries.

Emery--Mila verified pseudo-arithmeticity for the higher-dimensional Coxeter
groups available to them, and work published in 2026 still described all
then-known lattices in \(\PO(n,1)\), \(n>3\), as pseudo-arithmetic
\cite{BBKS}.  We know of no previously recorded non-pseudo-arithmetic lattice
in these dimensions.  This records a targeted literature search, rather than
claiming that any bounded search proves absolute priority.

\section{Trace fields and pseudo-arithmeticity}

Let \(n>3\), and let \(\Gamma<\PO(n,1)\) be a lattice.  Its \emph{adjoint
trace field} is
\[
 k(\Gamma)
 =\Q\bigl(\{\operatorname{tr}(\Ad\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma\}\bigr)
 \subset\R,
\]
where \(\Ad\) is the adjoint representation.  This is a number field.  Vinberg
proved that it is a commensurability invariant and that there is, up to
\(k(\Gamma)\)-isomorphism, a unique \(k(\Gamma)\)-form
\(\mathbf G_\Gamma\) of the relevant real projective orthogonal group for
which \(\Gamma\subset\mathbf G_\Gamma(k(\Gamma))\)
\cite{VinbergRings}.  The group \(\mathbf G_\Gamma\) is called the
\emph{ambient group} of \(\Gamma\).

For a number field \(k\subset\R\), a \(k\)-form \(\mathbf H\) of
\(\PO(n,1)\) is \emph{admissible} if
\(\mathbf H(k\otimes_\Q\R)^\circ\) has exactly one noncompact factor, and
that factor is isomorphic to \(\PO(n,1)^\circ\).  When \(n>3\), admissibility
forces \(k\) to be totally real.  Following Emery--Mila, a lattice with
adjoint trace field \(K\) is \emph{pseudo-arithmetic} if there are an
admissible \(k\)-group \(\mathbf H\) and a finite real multiquadratic extension
\[
 K=k(\sqrt{d_1},\ldots,\sqrt{d_t}),\qquad d_i\in k^\times,
\]
such that \(\mathbf G_\Gamma\cong_K\mathbf H_K\).  Here \emph{real} is part of
the pseudo-admissibility condition: all archimedean embeddings of \(K\) are
real.  The possibly trivial case \(t=0\) includes quasi-arithmetic lattices.

We will need only the following immediate obstruction.

\begin{proposition}[Emery--Mila]\label{prop:total-real-obstruction}
If \(n>3\) and \(\Gamma<\PO(n,1)\) is pseudo-arithmetic, then its adjoint trace
field \(k(\Gamma)\) is totally real.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the notation of the definition, the field \(k\) is totally real by
admissibility and \(K/k\) is a real multiquadratic extension.  Hence every
embedding \(K\hookrightarrow\C\) has image in \(\R\).  This consequence is
also stated explicitly in \cite[Section 1.4]{EmeryMila}.
\end{proof}

We next recall the reflection-group description of the same field.  Let
\(P\subset\mathbb H^n\) be a finite-volume Coxeter polytope with facets
\(F_0,\ldots,F_{N-1}\).  Choose outward unit spacelike normals \(e_i\), and
write
\[
 G=(g_{ij}),\qquad g_{ij}=\langle e_i,e_j\rangle .
\]
Thus \(g_{ii}=1\); if \(F_i,F_j\) meet at angle \(\pi/m_{ij}\), then
\(g_{ij}=-\cos(\pi/m_{ij})\); parallel facets have \(g_{ij}=-1\); and
ultraparallel facets at distance \(\rho_{ij}\) have
\(g_{ij}=-\cosh\rho_{ij}\).  Put \(A=2G=(a_{ij})\).  A \emph{cyclic product}
is an expression
\[
 a_{i_0i_1}a_{i_1i_2}\cdots a_{i_{m-1}i_m}a_{i_mi_0}.
\]
The \emph{Vinberg field} \(k(P)\) is the field generated over \(\Q\) by all
such products.

\begin{proposition}[Vinberg]\label{prop:vinberg-cycles}
If \(P\) has finite volume and \(\Gamma(P)\) is its reflection group, then
\[
 k(\Gamma(P))=k(P).
\]
\end{proposition}

This is the cyclic-product form of Vinberg's field-of-definition theorem
\cite{VinbergRings}; see also \cite[Section 4.1]{EmeryMila} and the modern
formulation in \cite[Section 3.1]{GuoMaZangZheng}.  The normalization matters
only for bookkeeping: Emery--Mila use the bilinear Gram matrix with diagonal
\(2\), which is precisely \(A=2G\) in the unit-normal convention above.

\section{The three Coxeter polytopes}

We use zero-based facet indices.  A Coxeter vector on seven facets records the
\(21\) pairs in lexicographic blocks:
\[
\begin{split}
 &(01,02,03,04,05,06)\mid(12,13,14,15,16)\mid(23,24,25,26)\\
 &\hspace{42mm}\mid(34,35,36)\mid(45,46)\mid(56).
\end{split}\tag{1}
\]
An integer \(m\ge2\) records a dihedral angle \(\pi/m\); the symbol \(0\)
records a parallel pair; and the letters \(a,b\) record ultraparallel pairs
with \(g_{ij}=-a,-b\), respectively.  Thus, for example, the initial block
\(2232ab\) says
\[
 m_{01}=2,\quad m_{02}=2,\quad m_{03}=3,\quad m_{04}=2,
 \quad g_{05}=-a,\quad g_{06}=-b.
\]

The three rows of the Ma--Zheng census are as follows.  The volume column is
the volume of the Coxeter fundamental polytope.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{cclc}
polytope & Coxeter vector & cusps & volume \\
\hline
\(P_{11,6}\) & \(\mathtt{2232ab\ 52322\ 2220\ 442\ 22\ 2}\)
    & \(1\) & \(13\pi^2/864\) \\
\(P_{11,7}\) & \(\mathtt{2232ab\ 52323\ 2220\ 442\ 22\ 2}\)
    & \(1\) & \(\pi^2/54\) \\
\(P_{11,8}\) & \(\mathtt{2232ab\ 52324\ 2220\ 442\ 22\ 2}\)

\end{tabular}

Ma--Zheng \cite{MaZheng}.}
\label{tab:three-polytopes}
\end{table}

Put
\[
 s=\sqrt5,\qquad r=\sqrt2.
\]
The three rows have the same first ultraparallel entry
\[
 a=\frac12\sqrt{7+s+2\sqrt{2+2s}}.\tag{2}
\]
For completeness, their other ultraparallel entries are
\[
\begin{aligned}
 b_6&=\sqrt{\frac92+2s+\sqrt{38+17s}},\\
 b_7&=r+\frac{\sqrt{10}}2+\frac12\sqrt{29+13s},\\
 b_8&=\frac14\left(2+3r+2s+rs
       +2\sqrt{19+14r+9s+6rs}\right).
\end{aligned}\tag{3}
\]
The positive radicals in (2)--(3) are the values tabulated by Ma--Zheng; in
particular \(a,b_j>1\), as required for ultraparallel facets.  Only the common
entry \(a\) will enter the obstruction.

Reading (1) from any of the three vectors gives
\[
 A_{12}=-2\cos\frac{\pi}{5}=-\frac{1+s}{2},\qquad
 A_{03}=-1,\qquad A_{35}=-r,\qquad A_{50}=-2a.\tag{4}
\]
Equations (2) and (4) contain all the Coxeter data used in the proof.

\section{The two-cyclic-product obstruction}

We isolate the elementary number-field calculation first.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:non-real-subfield}
Let \(s=\sqrt5\), let \(a\) be given by (2), and put \(u=\sqrt2\,a\).  Then
\[
 (s-1)u^2+2(s-3)u+s-7=0.\tag{5}
\]
Moreover, the number field \(L=\Q(s,u)\) is not totally real.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set \(t=\sqrt{2+2s}\).  From (2),
\[
 u^2=2a^2=\frac{7+s}{2}+t.
\]
Using \(s^2=5\), a direct calculation gives
\[
 \left(\frac{3-s+t}{s-1}\right)^2
 =\frac{7+s}{2}+t=u^2.
\]
Since \(1<s<3\) and \(t>0\), both \(u\) and
\((3-s+t)/(s-1)\) are positive.  Hence
\[
 (s-1)u=3-s+t.
\]
Squaring \(t=(s-1)u+s-3\), using \(t^2=2+2s\), and dividing by
\(s-1\ne0\) gives (5).

Consider (5) as a quadratic polynomial \(f(X)\in\Q(s)[X]\).  Its discriminant
is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \Delta
 &=4(s-3)^2-4(s-1)(s-7)\\
 &=8(1+s).
 \end{aligned}\tag{6}
\]
Let \(\sigma:\Q(s)\hookrightarrow\R\) be the nontrivial embedding,
\(\sigma(s)=-s\).  The conjugate polynomial \(f^\sigma\) has discriminant
\[
 \sigma(\Delta)=8(1-s)<0.\tag{7}
\]
In particular \(f^\sigma\) has no real root.

If \(u\) belonged to \(\Q(s)\), then the real number \(\sigma(u)\) would be a
root of \(f^\sigma\), contradicting (7).  Thus \(f\) is the minimal polynomial
of \(u\) over \(\Q(s)\).  Extending \(\sigma\) to \(L\) sends \(u\) to one of
the two nonreal roots of \(f^\sigma\).  Hence \(L\) has a complex place and is
not totally real.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Fix \(j\in\{6,7,8\}\), and put
\[
 K_j=k(\Gamma_{11,j})=k(P_{11,j}),
\]
where the equality is Proposition~\ref{prop:vinberg-cycles}.  From (4), two
cyclic products in \(A=2G\) are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c_{12}&=A_{12}A_{21}=\frac{3+s}{2},\\
 c_{035}&=A_{03}A_{35}A_{50}=-2ra.
 \end{aligned}\tag{8}
\]
Consequently
\[
 s=2c_{12}-3\in K_j,
 \qquad
 u=ra=-\frac12c_{035}\in K_j.
\]
Thus \(L=\Q(s,u)\) is a subfield of \(K_j\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:non-real-subfield}, \(L\) has a nonreal embedding
\(\iota:L\hookrightarrow\C\).  Since \(K_j/L\) is a finite separable
extension, \(\iota\) extends to an embedding \(K_j\hookrightarrow\C\).  Its
image cannot lie in \(\R\), because its restriction to \(L\) does not.  Hence
\(K_j\) is not totally real.

Proposition~\ref{prop:total-real-obstruction} now shows that
\(\Gamma_{11,j}\) is not pseudo-arithmetic.  The same argument applies to all
three values of \(j\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:manifolds}]
Let \(\Gamma=\Gamma_{11,j}\).  Its orientation-preserving subgroup
\(\Gamma^+=\Gamma\cap\Isom^+(\mathbb H^4)\) has index two.  Since a Coxeter
group is a finitely generated linear group, Selberg's lemma \cite{Selberg}
gives a torsion-free
finite-index subgroup \(\Lambda<\Gamma^+\).  Therefore
\[
 M_j=\Lambda\backslash\mathbb H^4
\]
is an orientable finite-volume hyperbolic \(4\)-manifold.  It is noncompact
because \(P_{11,j}\) has at least one ideal vertex.  Adjoint trace fields are
invariant under passage to finite-index subgroups \cite{VinbergRings}; hence
\(k(\Lambda)=k(\Gamma)=K_j\).  Since \(K_j\) is not totally real,
Proposition~\ref{prop:total-real-obstruction} shows that \(\Lambda\), and thus
\(M_j\), is not pseudo-arithmetic.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact certification and reproducibility}

The proof above is hand-checkable and requires neither a primitive element for
the full trace field nor a numerical signature calculation.  We nevertheless
record independent exact checks, both to validate the transcription of the
census rows and to make the examples reproducible.

\subsection{Gram matrices}

For each of the three rows, exact elimination verifies that the seven-by-seven
unit-normal Gram matrix has rank \(5\).  All seven principal six-by-six minors
vanish.  The principal block on facets \(0,1,2,3,4\) has leading principal
minors
\[
 1,\quad 1,\quad \frac{5-s}{8},\quad
 \frac{3(5-s)}{32},\quad -\frac{1+s}{32}.\tag{9}
\]
Thus this block has inertia \((4,1)\), and the full Gram matrix has exact
inertia
\[
 (n_+,n_-,n_0)=(4,1,2).
\]
The calculation also verifies (2)--(5) symbolically.  Ma--Zheng's
classification supplies finite volume for all three rows.

For \(P_{11,8}\), we additionally checked finite volume directly from the
principal subdiagrams.  Among the ten maximal elliptic or parabolic
subdiagrams, eight are elliptic of rank four.  The other two are the
rank-three parabolic diagrams on
\[
 \{1,3,4,6\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \{2,3,4,5,6\};
\]
their types are, respectively,
\(\widetilde C_3\) and
\(\widetilde A_1\sqcup\widetilde C_2\).  The latter has nullity two and is the
Euclidean triangular-prism link of the nonsimple ideal vertex.  Every one of
the \(21\) elliptic rank-three edge diagrams has exactly two ordinary or ideal
endpoints.  Vinberg's finite-volume criterion \cite{VinbergReflections}
therefore gives two cusps and finite covolume.  Independently, the pinned
CoxIter calculation \cite{Guglielmetti} returns
\[
 (f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3,f_4)=(10,21,18,7,1),\qquad
 \operatorname{vol}(P_{11,8})=\frac{19\pi^2}{864}.
\]

\subsection{A full-field check}

The two cycles in (8) are the smallest certificate, but the whole adjoint
trace field of \(P_{11,8}\) can also be recovered.  In addition to them, the
cycles
\[
 c_{126}=-r(1+s),\qquad c_{03416}=-4b_8
\]
successively recover \(r,a,b_8\).  Since every Gram entry belongs to
\(\Q(s,r,a,b_8)\), this proves
\[
 k(P_{11,8})=\Q(s,r,a,b_8).
\]
Set \(w=2(r+a+b_8)\).  Exact power-basis expressions for \(s,r,a,b_8\)
in terms of \(w\) show that \(w\) is primitive, so
\(k(P_{11,8})=\Q(w)\).  Its minimal polynomial is
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p_w(x)={}&x^8-8x^7-252x^6-1392x^5-3524x^4\\
          &-5280x^3-5712x^2-3968x-1136.
\end{aligned}\tag{10}
\]
An independent PARI/GP computation gives number-field signature
\((r_1,r_2)=(4,2)\) and discriminant
\[
 40960000=2^{16}5^4.
\]
In particular, the complete field computation agrees with the much shorter
complex-place certificate in Lemma~\ref{lem:non-real-subfield}.

\subsection{Artifacts and verification boundary}

The accompanying repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/sashakolpakov/PseudoArithmetic}}
contains the following exact verification artifacts:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \texttt{scripts/p11\_6\_7\_non\_total\_trace.py} and
 \texttt{scripts/p11\_8\_non\_total\_trace.py} reproduce the exact rank,
 inertia, cyclic-product, and field calculations; the latter invokes PARI/GP
 to check irreducibility, signature, and discriminant for (10);
 \item \texttt{scripts/p11\_8\_finite\_volume.py} exhausts the \(127\)
 nonempty principal submatrices and checks the vertex and edge conditions;
 \item \path{scripts/p118_trace_field.py} reconstructs the Vinberg form by a
 second primitive-element route, while
 \path{scripts/p118_field_independent.gp} checks irreducibility, the
 real-root count, the field signature, and the discriminant of the resulting
 alternative degree-eight polynomial;
 \item the corresponding machine-readable certificates are stored in
 \texttt{results/}, together with a pinned CoxIter input, captured output,
 and build provenance.
\end{itemize}
The Ma--Zheng TeX source used for the three rows has SHA-256
\[
\text{\texttt{\small
0017931637f017641d4935450cc2a07d070866d9c749443d9e3ede9f3a39f003}}.
\]
Each symbolic case is run in a fresh process; the observed peak resident set
size is below \(100\) MB.

The directory \texttt{formal/} is a pinned Lean~4/mathlib project.  It
kernel-checks the intrinsic algebraic statement that, for a number field \(K\)
and \(s,u\in K\),
\[
 \operatorname{minpoly}_{\Q}(s)=X^2-5
 \quad\hbox{and}\quad
 (s-1)u^2+2(s-3)u+s-7=0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 K\text{ is not totally real}.
\]
In particular, Lean obtains the embedding \(s\mapsto-\sqrt5\) from the
minimal polynomial and proves that it cannot be real.  A second module
kernel-checks the normalized Clifford and split-support implication chains
conditionally: ABHN, Hasse--Minkowski, local Brauer functoriality, and the
pseudo-arithmetic characterization occur as explicit theorem hypotheses,
not as new axioms.  The Ma--Zheng classification and radical data, the
concrete extraction of \(s,u\) from the Coxeter cycles, Vinberg's
cyclic-product theorem, and the Emery--Mila total-reality implication
likewise remain named inputs at the application boundary.  The development
contains
no project-defined axiom or opaque declaration and no \texttt{sorry} or
\texttt{admit}.
